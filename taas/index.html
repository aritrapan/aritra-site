Call Bridge

You + 3 computer players · no partners

1The deck: all 52 cards

In every suit, cards rank from Ace (highest) down to 2 (lowest):

highest ◀────────────────────▶ lowest

Spades ♠ are always trump. Any spade beats any card of another suit.

2Players

Four players and no partners: everyone plays for themselves. Each player gets 13 cards. Play goes anticlockwise: You → East → North → West.

3The call

🗣️Look at your 13 cards, then call how many tricks you expect to win, from 1 to 13. Each player calls once, in turn.
🎯Your call is your target for the round. Under each player you'll see tricks won / call, for example 2/4.

4Playing a trick

✅Follow the suit that was led if you can, with any card of that suit. You don't have to beat the card on the table.
🃏None of that suit? Play any card. A spade will usually win the trick, but you can throw away a card from another suit instead.
🏆The highest spade wins the trick. If no spade was played, the highest card of the suit led wins. The winner leads the next trick. Spades can be led at any time.

Only the cards you're allowed to play light up, so you can't break these rules by accident.

5Scoring each round

Made your call: + your call, plus 0.1 for each extra trick.
Fell short: − your call.

Called 4, won 6+4.2
Called 5, won 5+5.0
Called 5, won 3−5.0

6The game

A game is 5 rounds, and the highest total wins. The deal moves one seat anticlockwise each round.

7Difficulty

Easy: the computer follows fixed playing rules: cash sure winners, ruff when it can, and stop winning once its call is made.

Hard: before each card, the computer imagines up to 150 ways the unseen cards could be spread among the other players (consistent with everything it has seen, including who has run out of which suit), plays each one out, and picks the card that does best on average.

Hard also decides its call this way: it plays the round out on a few hundred imagined deals, counts how many tricks it tends to win, and calls the number with the best average score.

29

Kolkata-style rules · you + 3 computer players

1The deck: 32 cards

Only J, 9, A, 10, K, Q, 8, 7 of each suit are used. In every suit they rank from highest to lowest like this:

highest ◀────────────────────▶ lowest

The yellow numbers are points. Jack is boss (3), Nine comes second (2), Ace and Ten are worth 1 each. The deck holds 28 points in total, and every hand is a fight over them.

2Teams

You + North play against East + West. Your partner sits opposite you.

Play goes anticlockwise: You → East → North → West.

3Bidding and the secret trump

🂠Each player gets 4 cards to begin with.
🗣️Players bid between 16 and 28. A bid is the number of points your team promises to win. Each bid must be higher than the last. A player who passes is out of the auction. If the first three players all pass, the last player has to take the bid at 16.
🤫The highest bidder picks the trump suit in secret. Nobody else knows what it is yet.
✋Each player then gets 4 more cards, so everyone holds 8, and the 8 tricks are played.

4Playing a trick

✅You must follow the suit that was led if you can.
❓If you have none of that suit, you can play any card, or tap Ask for trump to have the trump revealed.
🃏The player who asked for trump must play a trump if they hold one. From that point on, trumps beat every other suit, and that includes the current trick.
🏆Until trump is revealed there are no trumps at all, and the highest card of the suit led wins the trick.

5The Pair (King + Queen of trump)

Suppose one player holds both the K and Q of trump when trump is revealed. The pair counts as soon as that player's team wins a trick afterwards:

Bidder's team holds it the target goes down by 4 (it can't go below 16).
Opponents hold it the target goes up by 4 (it can't go above 28).

6Scoring

If the bidding team reaches its target, it scores +1. If it falls short, it scores −1.

The first team to reach +6 wins the match, and a team that drops to −6 loses it.

This version keeps things simple: there's no double/redouble, no bonus for the last trick, and trump is chosen as a suit rather than shown with a face-down card.

7Difficulty

Easy: the computer follows fixed playing rules and keeps track of which cards have gone.

Hard: before each card, the computer imagines about 150 possible ways the unseen cards could be spread among the other players (consistent with everything it has seen), plays each one out to the end, and picks the card that does best on average. It also decides when to ask for trump this way.

Your partner plays at the same level. Each level keeps its own match record. A change takes effect from the next hand.

ℹ️ How it works

The host's device runs the game and the bots; everyone else's device shows only their own cards. Devices connect directly to each other. Free public servers only help them find each other, and the table code locks that step, so nobody without the code can join. Nothing is stored and no account is needed.

Keep this page open on the host's device: if it sleeps, the game pauses for everyone. If a friend drops out, a bot plays their seat until they rejoin with the same name. Some mobile networks block direct connections; if a friend can't connect, try Wi-Fi.
